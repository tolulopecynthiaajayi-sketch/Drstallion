Letter from the Editor  •  By Tolulope

Temitope, As I Fondly Call You.

To the rest of the world, you are TRB, Dr. Stallion, an executive leader and a formidable voice. To me, you will always be Temitope, the name given to you from birth and the name I fondly call you. Every time I say it, it is an echo of the truth that I am deeply thankful to God for you. You can probably tell that I have had a lot of fun putting this entire magazine together for you. It is your 39th birthday today, and looking at you, I am just grateful to God for everything.

Your heart is so big. It honestly annoys me sometimes because people take advantage of your kindness, yet would I have you any other way? Not really, though maybe just a little on the days you refuse to protect your own peace!

You give yourself so freely to others that I often wonder what you leave for yourself. I know you carry burdens that I might never totally understand, and that is fine. I do not need to understand every single detail to love you, support you, and stand firmly in your corner through it all.

“One of the things that I carry with me about you is that for me, you are my friend, and nothing else matters.”

I chose this picture of you because it captures the true richness of your personality. Your laugh is such a beautiful sound, and I pray with all my heart that you never lose cause to laugh like this. May your light never dim, and may you continue to shine in every space you enter.

Now, on a very serious note, please retire from playing football and become a coach instead. Coach is also a very good title, and your knees will thank you! ⚽ I know you will still find ways to annoy me, of course, yet what would life even be without that?

I pray for you that as you enter this new year, the Almighty God will carry you higher. May He lift your head above your peers and carry you upon His wings. As you celebrate 39 years today, you will do 40, 50, 60, 70, 80, 90, and 100 if Jesus tarries. You will live in great health, in wealth, and in honor all the days of your life.

Thank God for you, Temitope. I love you very much.

Tolulope
Yours always, with so much love

The Digital Mythos: Dr. Stallion

The 20-Hour Corporate Engine

In the collective imagination of social media, LinkedIn, and global corporate Dublin, "Dr. Stallion" is an unstoppable humanoid algorithm. He wakes at 4:30 AM to read global market filings while doing one-arm pushups. He reviews 400 executive CVs before breakfast, reorganizes departmental org charts with a single penetrating gaze, and creates flawless presentation decks at record speed without a drop of perspiration.

Colleagues describe him with awe for his sheer dedication and the astonishing speed with which he gets high-stakes work done. His turnaround time makes complex projects seem effortless, backed by an aura that suggests he has never experienced fatigue since 2011.

The Domestic Reality: 39-Year-Old Temi

The Ekiti Gentleman In The Fleece Blanket

Behind closed doors, the mythical stallion is fundamentally a 39-year-old Nigerian gentleman who wants nothing more than silence, real Ekiti pounded yam with steaming hot soup, and thirty minutes without someone tagging him in a video about "The Future of AI in Talent Acquisition." He has also elevated taking naps into an essential daily ritual, claiming restorative sleep at any given opportunity.

Come nightfall, however, the man almost never drops his phone. Well past 10:00 PM, his screen is still glowing as he attends to yet another message: "Doc, quick question on my career transition." He groans when rising from low armchairs, keeps four different brands of joint rub within reach, and rarely sleeps before 10:00 PM while claiming he wants to rest like an honorable elder statesman.

The 47-Bottle Fragrance Monograph

The Sillage of Dr. Stallion: Smelling Like A Diplomat Since 2011.

In corporate circles, presence is measured by charisma and strategic foresight. For Dr. Temitope Richard-Banji, it is also measured by the three-mile olfactory shockwave that heralds his arrival. Temi does not merely wear perfume; he conducts atmospheric alchemy.

His fragrance vault is an architectural wonder: high-concentration Extraits de Parfum, artisanal French flacons, rare Cambodi and Indonesian ouds, smoky amber blends, and spicy cardamom layers. A single spray is an insult to his philosophy; Dr. Stallion layers six distinct atomizers until his scent trail has its own postal code in Dublin.

Friends and colleagues confirm: you do not need Google Calendar to know Temi has entered the building. You simply inhale, detect notes of smoked vanilla, cedarwood, and Top 150 Global HR authority, and prepare yourself for an inspiring conversation.

What Temitope Smells Like to Me

Temitope owns an entire universe of bottles, yet truth be told, I have never paid attention to the fancy names on his shelf. I am not a perfume person, and he knows this.

Still, if I were asked to capture what Temitope smells like to me, it would have nothing to do with expensive labels. It would smell like quiet strength that does not need to shout. It would carry the gentle, protective stillness of someone who is deeply observant and cautious, paired with a fragile and tender heart that gives so much of itself. Above all, it would smell like steadfast loyalty, rooted like a tree that refuses to bend.

He can keep his collection of bottles; this quiet, faithful presence is the only fragrance that matters.

LFC Anfield Desk // YNWA Protocol

You'll Never Walk Alone (Because Your Cartilage Has Surrendered).

A forensic autopsy of the Saturday match he mistook his 39-year-old ankle for Mohamed Salah's.

Every Saturday across Dublin, an inspiring ritual unfolds. A respectable gentleman, an accomplished Ekiti scholar, and a Top 150 HR leader, Temitope wears his football player kit with unwavering pride. Rain or shine, summer chill or freezing winter frost, no matter the season, he is ready.

For sixty-four seconds on the pitch, Dr. Stallion looks like Steven Gerrard in his prime. He shouts tactical instructions at confused twenty-two-year-olds, points authoritatively into empty space, and plays with boundless passion. Yet football at thirty-nine obeys the merciless laws of orthopedic reality.

On minute sixty-seven, attempting a Mo Salah burst down the right wing, his right ankle emitted an acoustic vibration resembling dry spaghetti snapping inside a microphone. The match was halted. The dream of leading the front line was officially reconsidered.

At thirty-nine, staying injury-free is an Olympic discipline. As Tolulope wisely advised, the time has come to retire from playing football and become a coach instead; coach is also very good. Today, his ultimate matchday joy happens in supreme comfort: feet up, cozy blanket secured, and his official socks warning the entire household that Liverpool is on and nobody should dare interrupt.

The Struggle For The Algorithm

47 Takes, 5 Accents, and 1 Heroic Brand Manager

03:45 PM : The Daylight Epiphany

"The World Needs This Insight"

Fresh off a global consulting call and right before his afternoon recharge, inspiration strikes. The insight is profound: "Cross-functional synergy in remote teams is fundamentally an emotional intelligence bottleneck." He adjusts his spectacles. The world cannot wait until tomorrow. The ring light is powered on, his phone is locked into the tripod, and the studio is declared operational.

04:15 PM : The Technical Crisis

"Forehead Glare & The Slang Slip"

Take 8. The delivery is boardroom-ready, dripping with senior executive authority. Yet the studio ring light turns his forehead into an international lighthouse beam. He recalibrates the angle. Take 14: delivered with razor-sharp corporate cadence, until second 11 when an involuntary, heartfelt "abeg" escapes in the middle of a macroeconomic forecast. Delete.

04:45 PM : The 5-Way Accent War

"Ekiti, Akure, Lagos, UK, or Irish?"

Take 29. A ferocious geopolitical border war breaks out inside a single 60-second video. He begins with pristine London Mayfair diction, transitions into Dublin tech executive syntax, gets hijacked midway by classic Lagos Island corporate swagger, borrows acoustic warmth from Akure, and finishes with pure Ekiti determination. After trying to pronounce 'organizational' three different ways simultaneously, he pauses and stares blankly into the lens for 40 seconds.

05:25 PM : The Cloud Handoff

"Please Fix In Post (Bedtime at 10:00 PM)"

Temitope does not edit reels or write hooks himself. Thoroughly drained from staring at his own face in 4K, he dumps all 47 unedited raw takes into Google Drive. A voice note flies to his dedicated social media and brand manager: "Pick the best take, add captions, find a hook, and post it." Creative duties fulfilled, he winds down for the evening. By 10:00 PM sharp, he is fast asleep, resting peacefully while his brand person works digital miracles before morning.

The Wicked Stepmother of Asaba (Parts 1 to 4)

Rating: 10/10 Shouting

Plot was completely abandoned by minute seventeen, yet Chief Kanayo walked into the sitting room and yelled "Tufiakwa!" with such acoustic violence that my Samsung soundbar filed an industrial grievance. The sound mixer turned the background flute to maximum volume during an ordinary breakfast scene. Masterpiece.

Lekki Bloodline: The Return of the Billionaire

Rating: 10/10 Agitation

No one in this movie spoke below 90 decibels. Even the domestic staff communicated via operatic outrage. Whenever someone was betrayed, thunder cracked instantly inside a closed air-conditioned bedroom in broad daylight. This is the cinematic realism Dr. Stallion demands.

A Reasonable Discussion in Ikeja

Rating: 0/10 Disqualified

Two brothers sat down, poured tea, and resolved a multi-million-naira land dispute with rational, polite dialogue in twelve minutes. Unacceptable. No table was overturned. No one held their chest and collapsed. Disqualified from the canon.
