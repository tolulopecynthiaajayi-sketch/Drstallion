Letter from the Editor  •  By Cynthia Ajayi

The Quiet Heart Behind The Loud Accolades.

To the global corporate world, Temitope Richard-Banji is a formidable name. You can point to the Top 150 Global HR Influencer ranking, the thirteen solid years spent architecting talent strategy across continents, the keynote stages, and that unmistakable aura of executive authority that commands every room he enters.

Yet, those of us blessed to call him family know that his greatest title isn't 'Doctor' or 'Top 150 Influencer.' His greatest title is being the beating heart, the fierce protector, and the unyielding pillar of our family.

Flanked by a senior sister and a younger sister, Temi occupies a sacred, indispensable place in our sibling trio. To us, he is the ultimate brother: the one who sends a four-minute voice note critiquing your life choices with the gravitas of a Supreme Court justice, yet will immediately drop everything if you are distressed or in need. Whether he is welcoming us, our husbands, and our children into his beautiful home in Ikate for feasts and laughter, or dispensing executive wisdom across time zones, you can never feel alone in this world when Temitope is in your corner.

And though our beloved Mother now rests peacefully in the presence of the Lord, her legacy is alive in every fiber of who Temitope is. Her ceaseless prayers continue to serve as the divine shield over his life, career, and footsteps. Every standard of excellence he maintains, every act of quiet generosity he performs, and every room he enters with dignity is a direct reflection of the virtuous woman who raised him. She is looking down from heaven today with immense joy and pride, whispering blessings over her beloved son.

His Ekiti roots run deep in his intellectual stubbornness, his Christian faith anchors his soul, his love for Liverpool FC tests his cardiovascular resilience, his 47 bottles of niche perfume announce his presence before he even leaves his house, and his home in Ikate remains our sanctuary of warmth and joy.

Temi, as you turn 39, your sisters, your brothers-in-law, your nieces and nephews, and your entire family salute the extraordinary man you are. We thank God for your life and your boundless love. May this 39th year bring divine health, peace of mind, Liverpool silverware, fresh bottles of extrait, and renewed cartilage!

Cynthia Ajayi

The Digital Mythos: Dr. Stallion

The 20-Hour Corporate Engine

In the collective imagination of LinkedIn and corporate Dublin, "Dr. Stallion" is an unstoppable humanoid algorithm. He wakes at 4:30 AM to read labor market filings while doing one-arm pushups. He reviews 400 executive CVs before breakfast, reorganizes departmental org charts with a single penetrating gaze, and delivers visionary keynote remarks without a drop of perspiration.

He is described by colleagues as having "unfaltering bandwidth," an uncanny ability to resolve cross-border talent bottlenecks, and an aura that suggests he has never experienced fatigue since 2011.

The Domestic Reality: 39-Year-Old Temi

The Ekiti Gentleman In The Fleece Blanket

Behind closed doors, the mythical stallion is fundamentally a 39-year-old Nigerian gentleman who wants nothing more than silence, real Ekiti pounded yam with steaming hot soup, and thirty minutes without someone tagging him in a video about "The Future of AI in Talent Acquisition."

At 8:15 PM, his iPhone sits face down because if one more person messages "Doc, quick question on my career transition," his soul will exit his body. He groans when rising from low armchairs, possesses four different brands of joint rub, and will vigorously defend his right to sleep at 9:30 PM like an honorable elder statesman.

LFC Anfield Desk // YNWA Protocol

You'll Never Walk Alone (Because Your Cartilage Has Surrendered).

A forensic autopsy of the Sunday afternoon he mistook his 39-year-old ankle for Mohamed Salah's.

Every weekend across Dublin, a tragic comedy unfolds. A respectable man—an Ekiti scholar, a Top 150 HR leader with multiple postgraduate qualifications—puts on a full Liverpool FC red kit and genuinely convinces himself that Arne Slot has sent a scout undercover to an astro turf pitch in Tallaght.

For sixty-four seconds, Dr. Stallion looks like Steven Gerrard in his prime. He shouts tactical instructions at confused 22-year-olds; he points dramatically into empty space; he adjusts his captain's armband. But football at thirty-nine obeys the merciless laws of orthopedic physics.

On minute sixty-seven, attempting a Mo Salah burst down the right wing, his right ankle emitted an acoustic vibration resembling dry spaghetti snapping inside a microphone. The match was halted. The Liverpool dream was officially postponed until the next life.

The medical consensus was swift: the Reds are not signing a 39-year-old midfielder from Dublin. His recommended recovery protocol is sitting on the couch with ice, a hot bowl of pepper soup, and watching Liverpool on television while singing Allez Allez Allez from a safe reclining position.
